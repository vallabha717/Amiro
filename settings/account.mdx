Amiro has one account: the owner's. This page explains what that means for you
and how to manage it.

## What single-owner means

Amiro is built for one person on their own machine or server. The first account
you create is the **owner**. The owner is an admin, and admin-only pages (the
whole **Admin** section) are visible only to the owner.

There is no public sign-up. Once an owner exists, the setup page stops
accepting new owners and answers with an error forever. That is intentional: an
instance reachable over the network should not be claimable by a stranger.

<Note>
Provider connections belong to the instance, not to separate people. If you ever
want multiple untrusted users, that needs separate credentials and per-user
provider selection first. For now, treat everyone with access as the owner.
</Note>

## First run: creating the owner

There are two ways the owner gets created.

<Tabs>
<Tab title="In the app">
On a fresh instance, open Amiro and you land on the setup wizard. The **you**
step is the only required one. Enter a name, a login (an email or a username), a
password, and your timezone, and you are the owner. The password must be at
least 12 characters and include a letter plus at least one number or symbol.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Fully scripted">
If `AMIRO_OWNER_EMAIL` and `AMIRO_OWNER_PASSWORD` (or
`AMIRO_OWNER_PASSWORD_FILE`) are set when Amiro first starts with an empty user
store, the owner is created for you and the wizard skips the password step.
</Tab>
</Tabs>

Either way you then sign in with that email and password.

## Signing in

- Go to the sign-in page and enter your email and password.
- Your sign-in lasts for a while, so a page refresh does not sign you out. A
  server install defaults to a shorter session than the desktop app, which keeps
  you signed in for about a month.
- Sign-in attempts are rate-limited: after 15 tries in 15 minutes, further
  attempts are paused for a while. This protects a network-reachable instance.

<Warning>
There is no default password and no password reset by email. If you forget the
owner password, use the command line to reset it (see below).
</Warning>

## Changing your email or password

<Steps>
<Step title="Open Settings">
Open your profile menu at the bottom of the sidebar and choose **Settings**, then
**Account**.
</Step>
<Step title="Update the field">
Change your display name, email, or password and save.
</Step>
<Step title="Sign in again if your password changed">
A password change takes effect right away and signs out every other session. You
may need to sign in again on other devices.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Resetting a forgotten password

If you cannot sign in, reset the password from the machine running Amiro. The
command reads the new password from standard input (it is hidden when you type
it at a terminal), so it never appears in your shell history.

```bash
amiro reset-password                  # packaged server install (install.sh)
amiro-backend reset-password          # from a source checkout; asks twice
```

You can name the account explicitly with `--login <email or username>` if there
is more than one. Resetting the password signs out every session everywhere;
sign in again afterwards.

<Note>
`amiro` and `amiro-backend` must be the Amiro server programs for your install.
On a packaged server install, `amiro` is on your PATH and `amiro-backend` lives
under your Amiro data directory. On a desktop install the backend runs inside
the app; the exact binary paths are listed in [First run](/first-run).
</Note>

## Display name and how Amiro addresses you

Your display name is shown in the app and used in greetings. Your agent's own
name, avatar, and color are separate and live in **Agent → Customize**, not in
your account settings.
